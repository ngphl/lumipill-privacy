LumiPill Privacy Policy

Effective date: 25 October 2026

LumiPill is a medicine reminder app made by Phi Long Nguyen ("we"). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it. In short: your medicine list stays on your phone unless you choose to share it with family; if you turn on the Lock Screen countdown on iOS 17 or 18, the times of your next doses, never their names, go to our service so it can start the card; nothing is shared for advertising or tracking, and medicine photos leave your phone only if you choose to scan them and agree each time.

What stays on your phone

These are stored on your phone, and in your own iCloud or computer backup if you use one. They are never sent to us, except what you choose to share with family and the countdown's dose times (below):

  • your medicines, doses, times and instructions;
  • what you marked as taken or skipped;
  • copies of the photos you scanned, kept so you can compare a medicine with its original label;
  • photos you add to a medicine yourself (for example of its box or pills), which are never sent anywhere;
  • scan results you have not saved or discarded yet, with their photos, so you can finish checking them later; they are removed the first time you open Scan after 7 days;
  • your reminders, which the phone schedules itself;
  • your settings, such as the app's language;
  • a short log of recent scans (up to 40 entries of times and codes, with no medicine details), used to find faults;
  • the ticket of a scan still being read, so the app can collect its result if it was closed in the meantime.

We have no account system and no copy of this information. If you delete a medicine, its saved photos are deleted from the phone. If you delete the app, everything it stored is deleted, except a small access record in the iPhone's secure keychain (the access code below, with no medicine details), which iPhone keeps so the app can scan again if you reinstall it.

Scanning a label or prescription (optional)

Scanning is optional; you can always type a medicine in instead. When you scan:

  1. You take or choose photos. Before anything is sent, the app shows how many photos will be sent and asks you to agree. If you do not agree, nothing leaves your phone.
  2. The app makes a copy of each photo, at most 2048 pixels on its longest side, with its hidden details (such as location) removed, and sends only that copy over an encrypted connection.
  3. Our scanning service, run on Amazon Web Services in Sydney, Australia, reads the photos to find medicine names, doses and times:
    • Amazon Textract (Amazon Web Services) reads the printed text. We have opted out of Amazon using this content to improve its services.
    • OpenAI reads the photos and text to find the schedule details. OpenAI may keep a record of the request for up to 30 days for safety and abuse monitoring, and may process it outside Australia, including in the United States. OpenAI does not use data sent through its API to train its models, and we send it no information about who you are.
  4. The results come back to your phone for you to check. Nothing is added to your schedule until you have checked each medicine and saved it.

Amazon Web Services and OpenAI process your scans for us under their data processing terms, which protect your information as this policy does.

To get your photos to the scanning service, the app first uploads them over an encrypted connection to private, encrypted storage in our Amazon Web Services account in Sydney. Only our scanning service reads them there, and it deletes each photo as soon as it has read it, usually within seconds. If a scan is interrupted before that, the storage deletes the photos automatically within about two days. The service otherwise processes your photos in memory for that one scan. It does not keep the photos or the text read from them, and it keeps no scan history about you.

A scan can take a minute or two, so the service finishes it even if you switch apps or lose signal, and holds the result (the medicine names, doses and times it found) until your phone collects it. The result is encrypted with a key that only your phone has; our service does not keep that key, so it cannot read the result. It is deleted as soon as your phone has it, and if your phone never collects it, it can no longer be opened after one hour and is then erased automatically, usually within a few days.

Scan results can be wrong. Always compare them with the label or prescription before saving.

What our service does keep

To keep the scanning service working and to stop misuse, it keeps a small amount of technical information that contains no photos or medicine details:

  • a random access code that lets this app use the scanning service (stored on your phone, and on our service only in a scrambled form), and a random number the app creates for this installation, which includes the time it was created; both are kept for up to 90 days, then erased automatically, usually within a few days, and the number is also kept, as it is, with the invite you joined with, if any; neither is linked to your name or Apple ID;
  • if you joined with an invite, the invite (scrambled) and a count of your scans each month, so each tester stays within their monthly scans; if your phone set scanning up by itself, without an invite, a scrambled form of the installation number with a count of its scans each month, for the same reason; a month's count is erased after about two months;
  • a count of everyone's scans together each day and each month, with nothing about who made them, so the service's total stays within its limits; a day's count is erased after about two days, a month's after about two months;
  • a scrambled form of your connection's internet address and a count of recent scans and of phones setting scanning up, used to limit how often scanning can be used, kept for a limited time;
  • for seven days, then erased automatically, usually within a few days, technical records of each request, such as when it ran, how long it took, how many photos it had and whether it succeeded;
  • whether a scan is still running, finished or failed, next to its encrypted result (above): it can no longer be read after an hour, and is erased automatically, usually within a few days.

When you open Scan, the app checks with our service that scanning is available, sending this installation's random number; nothing is kept from that check.

Our service's database is backed up so it can be restored after a fault; these backups hold the same records (never photos), and those of family sharing and the Lock Screen countdown below, and are kept for up to 35 days.

Sharing your medicines with family (optional)

Family sharing is optional. If you choose "Share with someone" in More, Family, our service keeps a shared copy of your medicine list in Sydney, Australia, so the people you invite can follow it on their own phones. Before you share, the app shows what they will see, and our service records that you agreed, with the date and the version of the wording you saw.

While you share, our service keeps:

  • the times of your medicines, the dates each starts and ends, whether each is being taken, and your phone's time zone (kept up to date when you travel, so messages follow your clock);
  • what you tick each day (taken or skipped) and when you ticked it, for the last eight days and, for a time not ticked an hour after it, that it was not ticked;
  • a change a follower suggests for your list, until you add it or say no;
  • for each follower: the name they call you (for example "Bà"), the language they chose, their phone's time zone, which messages they asked for ("Tell me when"), and their phone's notification address (below);
  • for each phone, the access code it was given when it was connected, never your name, email or phone number.

The names of your medicines, their doses and instructions, and the names of the people who follow you are locked on the phones with a key that only the phones have (it travels in the invite), so we cannot read them. We never receive your photos.

The name a follower calls you is kept readable, so that a message on their phone can say who it is about ("Bà's 08:00 medicines are not ticked yet."). It is kept while they follow you and is never shown to anyone else.

Who sees it. Only the phones you invite, at most five, each with an invite that works one time, within 24 hours. If you tell LumiPill what family should call you, the invite carries that name, so it is filled in on their phone; it goes only in the invite you send, never to our service. You see everyone who follows you and can remove anyone. A follower sees your list and your ticks, and can choose to be told when a dose is taken, skipped or not ticked an hour after its time. A follower can suggest a change to your list; nothing changes on your phone until you add it.

Messages to followers. To tell a follower at once, even when LumiPill is closed on their phone, our service sends the message through Expo's push service (Expo, in the United States) and Apple's push notification service, to the follower phone's notification address. A message names you as the follower calls you and gives the time ("Bà took the 08:00 medicines."); it never contains a medicine's name or dose.

Stopping. You can stop sharing at any time. Our service then deletes your shared list, your ticks, the suggestions and the list of followers, usually within about two days, and the followers' phones remove their copy when they next check in. A follower can stop following at any time; their copy is removed from their phone, and the name they call you and their notification address are deleted.

Why. To show your family your medicines and ticks, and to tell them when a dose may have been missed, because you asked for it (your consent). You can withdraw your consent at any time by stopping sharing.

The Lock Screen countdown (optional)

If you turn on "Countdown on the Lock Screen" on an iPhone with iOS 17 or 18, the app sends our service, for your next doses (at most three, within the next day), the time an hour before each dose and the words the card will show, such as "Medicine at 08:00", "2 medicines" and "Still due", in the app's language, with where on your phone the card finds its picture. It also sends a code from Apple that lets our service start the card on your phone. It never sends a medicine's name or dose.

An hour before each dose, our service sends the card's start straight to Apple's push notification service, which starts it on your phone. Our service deletes these details a day after your last dose time (usually within a few days after that), and at once when you turn the countdown off or your reminders are off. On iOS 26 and later your phone starts the card itself and nothing is sent.

Why. To show the countdown you asked for while LumiPill is closed.

Testing LumiPill with TestFlight

If you test LumiPill through Apple's TestFlight app, Apple runs the test under its own privacy policy and shares with us the name and email address you were invited with (if you were invited by email), crash reports, basic usage information (such as when the app was installed and opened, your device model and system version) and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send. We use these only to run the test and fix problems. They never include your medicines or photos unless you put them in a screenshot or message yourself.

On the App Store, if you have agreed in your iPhone's settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may also send us crash reports and usage statistics for LumiPill that do not identify you. We use them only to fix problems.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera and photos: only to take or choose photos you want to scan, or a photo you add to a medicine yourself.
  • Notifications: to remind you at your medicine times, and, if you leave the app while a scan is running, to tell you when it should be ready (this alert never names a medicine). Both are scheduled on your phone; we do not send them. If you follow someone in family sharing, our service sends you their messages (above). If you turn on the Lock Screen countdown on iOS 17 or 18, our service starts it (above). A reminder shows the medicine's name and dose, and can appear on your lock screen; to hide it there, set Notifications, Show Previews to "When Unlocked" in the iPhone's Settings.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell your information, or share it with anyone except the services above that read your scans, deliver family messages and start the countdown, and the family you choose to share with.
  • We do not use advertising, tracking, or analytics tools of our own.
  • We do not give medical advice. LumiPill shows the schedule you confirm; it does not tell you what to take.

Children

LumiPill is not intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information about them.

Your choices

  • Use the app without scanning by typing medicines in.
  • Decline the photo-sending question at any time; nothing is sent.
  • Share with family only if you want to, and stop at any time in More, Family.
  • Turn reminders, camera or photo access off in the iPhone's Settings.
  • Delete a medicine, or the app, to remove what it stored on your phone.
  • Ask us about your information, or to delete it, at the email address below. Our service's records are not linked to your name, so we usually cannot tell which are yours; they are erased on their own as described above.

Emails to us

If you email us, we use your email address and message only to reply to you, and delete them when they are no longer needed.

Changes to this policy

If what LumiPill does with your information changes, we will update this page and its effective date before the change takes effect.
